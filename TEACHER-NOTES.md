# Teacher science and revision notes

Reviewed October 8, 2026. This version intentionally revises the supplied answer key where wording or evidence was weak. Explanations appear after both correct and incorrect responses and remain available in the final review. Peer-reviewed trials/reviews support health claims; FDA/NIH/USDA references support composition, labeling, and educational facts. These source types are labeled honestly in the app.

## Question-by-question changes

### 1. You want to replace butter with a fat that is mostly unsaturated. Which swap best fits that goal?
Correct response: Olive oil

Replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fat can lower LDL cholesterol, which is involved in artery plaque. What replaces the saturated fat matters: swapping in unsaturated oils is different from adding refined sugar. Think about the overall eating pattern, not a single “good” or “bad” food.

- [Sacks et al., Circulation (2017) · Scientific advisory](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28620111/)

### 2. Which of these has the highest proportion of saturated fat?
Original: Which of the following does not contain unsaturated fats?
Change: Corrects the false claim that butter contains no unsaturated fat.
Correct answer: Butter

Butter contains both saturated and unsaturated fats; it is not free of unsaturated fat. Compared with olive oil, it has a higher proportion of saturated fat. In a four-week randomized trial in adults, butter raised LDL cholesterol more than olive oil.

- [Khaw et al., BMJ Open (2018) · Randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29511019/)

### 3. Which snack supplies mostly unsaturated fat and no dietary cholesterol?
Original: Which of the following foods is least likely to raise your cholesterol levels?
Change: Replaces a broad prediction of individual cholesterol response with a food-composition question.
Correct answer: Peanuts

Peanuts supply mostly unsaturated fat. In a controlled feeding study, a diet including peanuts and peanut butter lowered LDL cholesterol compared with a typical higher-saturated-fat diet. That result concerns the whole diet; one snack does not guarantee a particular cholesterol level.

- [Kris-Etherton et al., AJCN (1999) · Controlled feeding trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10584045/)
- [FDA · Dietary cholesterol fact sheet](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/interactivenutritionfactslabel/assets/InteractiveNFL_Cholesterol_October2021.pdf)

### 4. Rank these foods from least to most cholesterol per gram of edible food.
Change: Replaces the two-food match with a five-food ascending ranking by cholesterol concentration. Food preparation and edible portion are specified. Reference values are approximate and apply to the listed USDA entries.
Correct response: Apple → white tuna (canned in oil, drained) → turkey breast (meat only, roasted) → ground beef patty (90% lean, broiled) → chicken leg (meat and skin, roasted).

For the specified foods, approximate cholesterol concentrations are: apple 0.00; white tuna 0.31; turkey breast 0.80; ground beef patty 0.88; and chicken leg with skin 1.27 mg per gram. The USDA table gives the four animal foods per 3 oz (about 85 g); dividing cholesterol by food weight makes the comparison fair. These are reference values, not constants for every recipe. Dietary cholesterol concentration does not by itself rank a food’s overall healthfulness or predict your blood cholesterol.

- [USDA SR Legacy (2018) · Cholesterol composition table; values converted to mg/g](https://www.nal.usda.gov/sites/default/files/page-files/cholesterol.pdf)
- [FDA · Dietary cholesterol fact sheet](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/interactivenutritionfactslabel/assets/InteractiveNFL_Cholesterol_October2021.pdf)

### 5. A study found similar hydration measures with moderate coffee and water intake in men who regularly drank coffee. Which conclusion is justified?
Correct response: Moderate coffee can contribute to hydration in habitual adult coffee drinkers.

Coffee contains water. A small crossover trial in 50 adult men who regularly drank coffee found similar hydration measures with moderate coffee intake and water. That does not establish the same result for everyone or make caffeine a recommendation for teens; it disproves the blanket “always dehydrates” claim.

- [Killer et al., PLOS ONE (2014) · Crossover trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24416202/)

### 6. Which choices can fit a DASH-style eating pattern that supports healthy blood pressure?
Original: To help lower blood pressure, which of these foods should you eat?
Change: Changes a guaranteed food effect to an evidence-supported dietary pattern.
Correct answer: All three

The DASH trial tested an overall eating pattern rich in fruits, vegetables and low-fat dairy, with less saturated fat. It lowered blood pressure in adults. Bananas, beans and no-salt-added tomato sauce fit that pattern; no single food guarantees a drop in blood pressure.

- [Appel et al., NEJM (1997) · DASH randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9099655/)

### 7. Which three-step plan helps you organize food shopping on a budget?
Original: What are the three P’s for eating healthy on a budget?
Change: Keeps the intended answer, labels it as practical guidance rather than peer-reviewed science.
Correct answer: Plan, purchase, prepare

Plan meals around your budget and what you already have, purchase what you need, then prepare those foods. This is a practical planning mnemonic, not a biological law or a clinical-trial result. USDA meal-planning guidance supports checking supplies and planning purchases.

- [USDA MyPlate · Practical meal-planning guidance](https://www.myplate.gov/eathealthy/budget/budget-weekly-meals)

### 8. An adult describes their drinking as “moderate.” Which statement best reflects the research on cancer risk?
Correct response: Some cancer risks can increase even at low intake; “moderate” does not mean risk-free.

A meta-analysis linked light drinking with increased risk of certain cancers, including breast cancer. Risk depends on the disease and amount consumed; a “moderate” category is not a promise of safety. This question teaches risk literacy, not a drinking target.

- [Bagnardi et al., Annals of Oncology (2013) · Meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22910838/)

### 9. Supermarket challenge: sort each product by its main grain ingredient.
Change: Replaces descriptive grain names with four named U.S. supermarket products. Students classify the main grain ingredient; product names contain no whole/refined hint.
Correct response: Whole main grain: Original Cheerios and Triscuit Original. Refined main grain: RITZ Original and Kellogg’s Rice Krispies.

Original Cheerios lists whole-grain oats first, and Triscuit Original starts with whole-grain wheat. RITZ Original starts with enriched wheat flour, while Rice Krispies starts with rice rather than brown or whole-grain rice. The first grain ingredient is the clue: enriched does not mean whole grain, and a product being a cracker or cereal does not tell you the answer. This classification concerns the main grain ingredient in the cited U.S. versions, not every ingredient or the product’s overall nutritional quality. Recipes can change; check your package.

- [General Mills · Original Cheerios product ingredients](https://www.cheerios.com/products/original-cheerios)
- [Mondelēz · Triscuit ingredients](https://www.mondelezinternational.com/our-brands/triscuit/index.html)
- [Snackworks / Mondelēz · RITZ Original ingredient list](https://www.snackworks.com/products/ritz-original-crackers-snacks-for-kids-and-adults-lunch-snacks-34-oz/)
- [WK Kellogg · Rice Krispies U.S. SmartLabel ingredient list](https://smartlabel.wkkellogg.com/Product/Index?gtin=00038000199943)
- [FDA · Whole-grain definition (draft guidance)](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/draft-guidance-industry-and-fda-staff-whole-grain-label-statements)

### 10. Food A has a glycemic index of 40 and Food B has a GI of 70, measured on the same glucose-reference scale. Which conclusion follows from GI alone?
Change: Replaces easy distractors with equal-weight, glycemic-load and percentage-rise misconceptions.
Correct response: With equal amounts of available carbohydrate, A produces a lower average two-hour blood-glucose response than B.

GI compares the area of the blood-glucose rise above baseline over about two hours, using equal amounts of available (digestible) carbohydrate and a reference food. It does not compare equal weights of food, and 40 or 70 is not the percent rise above a person’s starting glucose. Glycemic load also depends on how much available carbohydrate is in the portion, so usual serving sizes could reverse that comparison. GI values describe average test responses; individuals and mixed meals can differ.

- [Jenkins et al., AJCN (1981) · Human feeding study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6259925/)

### 11. You have 30 minutes before a hard interval workout. Each snack supplies about 250 calories and comes with a small cup of water. Assuming no food allergies or known intolerances, which would generally pose the greatest digestive-comfort challenge?
Change: Replaces explicitly high-fat wording with four plausible snacks, a common calorie level and the same 30-minute pre-exercise interval.
Correct response: A nut-and-seed trail mix with dried fruit

The nut-and-seed trail mix is generally the most concentrated source of fat and fiber here. Those can make a snack harder to tolerate close to intense exercise; dried fruit can add to the digestive challenge for some people. The other choices are generally more carbohydrate-focused and lower in fat. Matching calories does not match digestion time. This is a relative risk judgment, not a guarantee: portion details, hydration, intensity and personal tolerance matter. Trail mix can fit well at other times.

- [Thomas et al., MSSE (2016) · Sports nutrition position statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26891166/)
- [de Oliveira et al., Sports Medicine (2014) · Review of exercise-related gastrointestinal symptoms](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4008808/)

### 12. A label lists 150 mg sodium per serving. You eat two servings. How much sodium did you eat?
Original: In order to maintain a healthy diet and weight, what should you most pay attention to while preparing meals?
Change: Replaces a weight-focused oversimplification with food-label numeracy.
Correct answer: 300 mg

Nutrition Facts amounts are usually listed per serving. Two servings provide twice the listed amount: 150 × 2 = 300 mg. A labeled serving size describes an amount people typically consume; it is not a prescription for how much every person should eat.

- [FDA · Nutrition Facts label guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-facts-label/serving-size-nutrition-facts-label)

### 13. After a sugary snack, about how many minutes can a plaque-acid attack last? Set the clock to the approximate duration used in ADA classroom guidance.
Correct response: 20 minutes

About 20 minutes is the teaching estimate used in the ADA classroom resource. Plaque bacteria use sugars to produce acids that can remove minerals from enamel. This is not an exact cutoff: timing depends on the food, saliva, plaque and repeated eating or sipping. Saliva helps neutralize acid and restore minerals; a single snack does not automatically create a cavity. Repeated acid attacks can lead to tooth decay over time.

- [American Dental Association · Plaque Attack classroom resource (20-minute estimate)](https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/mouthhealthy/files/smile-smarts/lifetime_module02_plaque_attack.pdf)
- [Moynihan & Kelly, J Dental Research (2014) · Systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24323509/)
- [NIH / NIDCR · Tooth-decay mechanism](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/tooth-decay/more-info/tooth-decay-process)

### 14. A chromium supplement ad says it will “control blood sugar and stop sugar cravings.” What is the strongest scientific reason to question this claim?
Correct response: Blood-sugar results do not establish a craving benefit in healthy teens; that specific claim needs direct evidence.

The ad joins two different claims: blood-sugar control and fewer cravings. Studies have often focused on adults with type 2 diabetes, with mixed results. An effect in one population or on one measurement does not prove a craving benefit in healthy teens. A proposed biological mechanism is a reason to study a supplement, not proof that taking more produces a useful effect. The ad needs evidence for its specific claim and audience.

- [Costello et al., Nutrition Reviews (2016) · Review of trials](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27261273/)
- [NIH Office of Dietary Supplements · Chromium evidence summary](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Chromium-HealthProfessional/)

### 15. You use 150 g of the raw ground beef shown. About how many grams of fat does that portion contain?
Correct response: 30 g

The package says 20% fat by weight. For a 150 g raw portion, 0.20 × 150 = 30 g of fat. The 80% lean part is not 80% pure protein: it includes water and other components. The percentages describe the raw product; cooking can change its weight and composition. The image is a generated classroom package, not a real brand.

- [USDA FSIS · Lean/fat labeling guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/policy/fsis-directives/7130.1)

### 16. Two recovery meals provide the same calories and carbohydrate, but one has much more protein. What additional role does that protein most directly serve after resistance exercise?
Correct response: Supplying amino acids for muscle protein synthesis and remodeling

Protein supplies amino acids used to build and remodel muscle proteins. Because the two meals already supply equal carbohydrate, their difference in protein is not primarily about replacing glycogen fuel. Sweat-electrolyte replacement depends on minerals such as sodium and potassium; antioxidant vitamins have other roles. A protein-rich food may contain those nutrients too, but they do not explain the distinctive role of its protein. Exercise, total food intake and recovery all matter.

- [Stokes et al., Nutrients (2018) · Research review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29414855/)

### 17. Which food is a rich source of the long-chain omega-3 fats EPA and DHA?
Original: Which of the following foods is found to prevent eye disease?
Change: Corrects the claim that fish prevents eye disease; distinguishes food composition from trial outcomes.
Correct answer: Salmon

Fatty fish such as salmon supply EPA and DHA. DHA is part of retinal cell membranes, but that does not mean eating fish guarantees prevention of eye disease. In AREDS2, adding omega-3 supplements did not significantly reduce progression to advanced macular degeneration in older adults at risk.

- [NIH Office of Dietary Supplements · Omega-3 evidence summary](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Omega3FattyAcids-HealthProfessional/)
- [AREDS2 Research Group, JAMA (2013) · Randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23644932/)

### 18. What is the general daily sodium limit in FDA guidance for people age 14 and older?
Original: You should limit sodium intake to how much per day?
Change: Uses milligrams of sodium, states the age range, and separates sodium from salt.
Correct answer: Less than 2,300 mg

About one teaspoon of table salt contains 2,300 mg of sodium. Salt and sodium are not the same substance. The limit covers sodium from all foods and drinks, not just salt sprinkled on a meal; packaged and prepared foods are major sources.

- [FDA · Sodium guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-education-resources-materials/sodium-your-diet)

### 19. Choose two salad additions that are sources of mostly unsaturated fats.
Original: To avoid trans- fat, you should use what on your salads?
Change: Keeps choose-two interaction, replaces an ambiguous trans-fat/dressing comparison.
Correct answer: Walnuts + Olive oil

Walnuts and olive oil supply mostly unsaturated fats, while butter has a larger saturated-fat share. Replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fat can help lower LDL cholesterol. A “low-fat” label alone does not tell you a food’s trans-fat content.

- [Sacks et al., Circulation (2017) · Scientific advisory](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28620111/)

### 20. Which USDA visual introduced in 2011 shows food groups on a plate?
Original: What visual guide is currently used as a healthy outline of what to eat each day?
Change: Dates the question rather than claiming MyPlate is the current federal guide.
Correct answer: MyPlate

MyPlate is the plate-shaped educational graphic introduced in 2011. It depicts fruits, vegetables, grains and protein foods, with dairy alongside. This is a question about the named teaching tool and its history, not a claim that one graphic is a clinical study or a personalized eating plan.

- [USDA · MyPlate style guide, history and food groups](https://www.myplate.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/MyPlate-Style-Guide-2020-2025.pdf)

### Cholesterol calculation audit

USDA SR Legacy (2018) table values are 26 mg cholesterol per 3 oz for white tuna canned in oil, drained solids; 68 mg per 3 oz for roasted turkey breast, meat only; 75 mg per 3 oz for broiled 90%-lean ground beef patty; and 108 mg per 3 oz for roasted chicken leg, meat and skin. Using 3 oz = approximately 85.05 g gives 0.31, 0.80, 0.88 and 1.27 mg/g, respectively. The apple is 0 mg/g, consistent with FDA dietary-cholesterol guidance. These rounded source values have distinct ranks; they are not measurements of every possible brand or preparation. This is food composition, not an overall healthy/unhealthy ranking.

Product ingredient lists were checked October 8, 2026. Ingredient sourcing/classification uses manufacturer information, not clinical trials; the physiology questions retain peer-reviewed research sources. Brand names are used for classroom identification.


## Match-three edition

Questions now power an original 6×6 match-three board. First attempts award 3 moves for correct answers or 1 for an incorrect answer; a newly mastered comeback awards 2 moves once. Repeated incorrect comebacks award no moves. Students see the science explanation after either outcome. Learning XP and first-attempt accuracy remain separate from candy points. The board uses emoji treats, adjacent swaps, horizontal/vertical runs of 3+, gravity, refills, cascade bonuses and a 1,000-point level threshold. No external game assets or Candy Crush branding are used.

Question 13 restores an approximate 20-minute answer using ADA educational guidance while explicitly explaining timing variability and enamel remineralization. The interactive clock supports pointer dragging, tapping, arrow keys and a synchronized range input/number field. Question 15 now includes a generated 80/20 package photograph and requires applying the ratio to 150 g of raw beef. The chromium and protein questions distinguish mechanisms, outcomes and study populations instead of using implausible distractors. Other obvious distractors in questions 1, 5 and 8 were tightened as well.
